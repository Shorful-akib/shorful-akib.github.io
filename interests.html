Research Interests

Areas of Focus

The methods that anchor my work, the domains I've applied them to, and the direction I'm looking to extend into next.

Bayesian & Empirical Bayes Methods

Probabilistic modeling that formally incorporates prior knowledge and quantifies uncertainty, paired with empirical Bayes techniques that estimate prior distributions directly from data to stabilize noisy, sparse estimates.

Hierarchical & Small-Area Estimation

Borrowing strength across related units — age groups, regions, or survey domains — to model sparse, indirectly-observed outcomes like age-specific mortality. Recent work uses R-INLA (comparing exchangeable IID and age-structured RW2 priors), and extending toward full MCMC-based estimation in Stan to work with a broader model class.

Statistical Machine Learning

Supervised and unsupervised methods — ensembles, clustering, dimensionality reduction — as a complement to my classical statistical training.

Demography & Health Statistics

Quantitative methods for estimating mortality and population health outcomes from household survey data, including DHS-based adult mortality estimation and analyses of health service utilization and disparities.

Future Direction

Extending small-area estimation from age-structured smoothing toward explicitly spatial (geographic) random-effects models — e.g., CAR/ICAR structures — to capture cross-region borrowing of strength alongside cross-age.

Open to PhD research opportunities in Bayesian statistics, biostatistics, and demographic/health data science.
