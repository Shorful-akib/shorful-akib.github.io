Network-Adjusted Empirical Bayes Estimation of Age-Specific Adult Mortality from DHS Sibling Survival Histories

Motivation: Adult mortality is difficult to measure directly in countries with incomplete vital registration. DHS sibling survival histories offer indirect evidence, but estimates are noisy at fine age resolution, and a sibling's chance of being reported depends on how many survey-eligible sisters she has — a differential-visibility bias that can distort raw estimates.

Methodology: A network-based visibility adjustment (following Feehan & Borges, 2021) corrects each respondent's contribution before a heteroscedastic empirical Bayes (SURE) shrinkage step borrows strength across age-sex cells, stabilizing estimates in a sparse survey setting.

Data: 2024 Nigeria DHS sibling survival histories.

Status: Preprint — Research Square; under review, Population Health Metrics. First and corresponding author.

Exchangeable versus Age-Structured Priors in Small-Area Estimation of Adult Mortality from Sibling Survival Histories

Motivation: Applied small-area models routinely default to either an exchangeable or an age-structured smoothing prior, often without explicit justification. Whether this choice materially changes the resulting estimates — and under what conditions — had not been directly tested for survey-based adult mortality estimation.

Methodology: Compares SURE-based empirical Bayes shrinkage against two Bayesian Fay–Herriot models fitted via INLA (an exchangeable IID prior and an age-structured RW2 prior), applied in parallel to visibility-adjusted and unadjusted DHS estimates. A Monte Carlo simulation study, including boundary-age and input-contamination robustness checks, validates the mechanism under known ground truth.

Data: 2024 Nigeria DHS and 2016 Nepal DHS sibling survival histories, chosen to span a wide range of cell-level data sparsity.

Status: Preprint — SocArXiv; under review, Demography. First and corresponding author.

Decomposing Urban–Rural Inequalities in Maternal Health Care Utilization in Bangladesh

Motivation: Maternal health service utilization differs sharply between urban and rural Bangladesh; understanding the drivers of this gap informs targeted policy.

Methodology: Decomposition analysis applied to nationally representative health survey data.

Status: Published — Heliyon.

Health Literacy and Associated Factors Among University Students in Bangladesh

Motivation: Health literacy shapes health-seeking behavior; university students are an understudied population in Bangladesh.

Methodology: Survey design, data collection, and statistical analysis of associated factors, supported by a systematic review of existing literature.

Status: Forthcoming, BMC Public Health (co-first author); related systematic review under review.
